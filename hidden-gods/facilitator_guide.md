## 🎯 The Facilitator’s Role

In **Hidden Gods**, the **Facilitator** (formerly the MC or GM) is **not a traditional Game Master**. Instead, they are a **guide of possibilities**, responsible for:
- **Guiding the narrative** without controlling it.
- **Asking provocative questions** to deepen the story.
- **Introducing anomalies and other wonders**.
- **Playing NPCs and threats**.
- **Maintaining the world’s consistency**.
- **Ensuring everyone has a voice**.

> *"The Facilitator is the riverbank, not the river. The players are the current."*

---

## 📜 Facilitator Principles

These principles are **adapted from Apocalypse World** and tailored for Hidden Gods:

| Principle | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Barf Forth Hidden Gods-ica** | Describe the world **vividly and strangely**. | "The Debug Layer shimmers like a broken mirror, its code flickering in the air like fireflies." |
| **Address the Characters, Not the Players** | Speak to the **alter egos**, not the people playing them. | "The Navigator’s voice echoes in *The Creator’s* mind, not yours." |
| **Make Your Move, But Misdirect** | Be clear about **what’s happening**, but mysterious about **why**. | "The door creaks open on its own. You don’t see anyone there." |
| **Look Through Crosshairs** | Focus on **what’s immediately important**. | Ignore the distant lore; focus on the anomaly in front of the party. |
| **Name Everyone, Make Everyone Human** | Even minor characters should **feel real**. | The bartender isn’t just a bartender—she’s *Mira, who lost her brother to the Whispering Stones*. |
| **Ask Provocative Questions** | Push the players to **think deeply**. | "What does the symbol on the door remind you of?" |
| **Respond with Fuckery and Intermittent Rewards** | Keep things **interesting and unpredictable**. | The anomaly grants a boon but also a curse. |
| **Be a Fan of the Characters** | Root for their success, even as you **challenge them**. | Celebrate their clever solutions, even if they disrupt your plans. |
| **Think Offscreen Too** | Consider **what’s happening beyond the immediate scene**. | "While you argue, the River That Flows Uphill begins to rise." |
| **Sometimes, Disclaim Decision-Making** | Let the **players or the world decide**. | "The anomaly reacts to your touch. What does it do?" |

## ⚔️ Facilitator Moves

When the players **miss a roll** or you need to **advance the story**, make one of these moves. These are **tools to keep the narrative engaging and dynamic**.

### **Threat Moves**
| Move | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Separate them** | Split the group up. | "The door slams shut behind you, trapping you inside." |
| **Capture someone** | Take a character out of the scene. | "The shadows coil around *The Explorer* and drag them into the dark." |
| **Put someone in a spot** | Create a **difficult choice**. | "The bridge is collapsing. Do you save the NPC or your ally?" |
| **Trade harm for harm** | Escalate the violence. | "The anomaly lashes out, dealing 2-Harm to everyone in the room." |
| **Announce off-screen badness** | Something bad happens **elsewhere**. | "While you celebrate, the Debug Layer’s core begins to destabilize." |
| **Announce future badness** | Foreshadow **coming trouble**. | "The Navigator whispers: *The storm is coming.*" |

### **Opportunity Moves**
| Move | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Inflict harm** | As established by the rules. | "The anomaly’s touch burns you. Take 1-Harm." |
| **Take away their stuff** | Remove a **resource or advantage**. | "Your map dissolves into the psychic maelstrom." |
| **Make them buy** | Offer something **at a cost**. | "The door will open if you leave your weapon behind." |
| **Activate their stuff’s downside** | Trigger a **negative aspect** of their gear or abilities. | "Your psychic amplifier overloads. Take 1-Weird harm." |
| **Tell them the possible consequences and ask** | Present **options**. | "The anomaly hums. Do you touch it or walk away?" |
| **Offer an opportunity, with or without a cost** | Give them a **chance**, maybe with strings attached. | "The Navigator offers you a vision—but it will cost you a memory." |
| **Turn their move back on them** | Use their **action against them**. | "Your attempt to hack the code triggers a firewall that locks you out." |

## 🗺️ Threat Map

The **threat map** is your tool for **tracking the world’s dangers and opportunities**. It helps you **visualize the forces** at play in your world.

### **How to Create a Threat Map**
1. Draw a **large circle** on a piece of paper or digital canvas.
2. Place the **PCs and their resources** in the **center**.
3. Add **closer threats** in the **next ring** (immediate dangers).
4. Add **farther threats** in the **outer ring** (distant dangers).
5. Add **notional threats** **outside the circle** (ideas not yet introduced).

### **Threat Types**
| Type | Impulse | Example | Description |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Warlords** | To control, dominate | A tyrant ruling a nearby hold | A despot who seeks to conquer the layer. |
| **Grotesques** | To distort, consume | A mutant stalking the wastes | A being that warps reality around it. |
| **Brutes** | To destroy, victimize | A raiding party, a wild beast | A group or creature that attacks directly. |
| **Afflictions** | To spread, weaken | A plague, a curse, a delusion | A force that infects or weakens the party. |
| **Landscapes** | To contain, challenge | A maze, a fortress, a prison | A physical obstacle or environment. |
| **Vehicles** | To move, destroy | A war machine, a ghost ship | A mobile threat or opportunity. |
| **Anomalies** | To reveal, transform | A whispering stone, a river flowing uphill | A mystery or wonder that changes the world. |

### **Threat Template**
Each threat should have:
1. **A name** (e.g., *The Debugger’s Wrath*).
2. **A type** (e.g., *Affliction*).
3. **An impulse** (e.g., *To corrupt the party’s understanding of the code*).
4. **A description and cast** (e.g., *The Debugger’s glitches manifest as floating symbols that rewrite reality*).
5. **Stakes questions** (e.g., *What happens if the party fails to resist the corruption?*).
6. **Custom moves** (e.g., *The symbols begin to replace the party’s memories with false ones*).

## 🔄 Maintaining Continuity

As the saga grows, **maintaining continuity** becomes important. Use these tools to keep your world consistent:

### **1. Review the World Bible**
Before each session, **review the World Bible** to remind yourself of:
- **Established lore** (layers, gods, anomalies).
- **Character details** (archetypes, parts, relationships).
- **Ongoing arcs** (mysteries, conflicts, or goals).

### **2. Check Character Journals**
Ask players to **share their character journals** (if they keep them) to understand:
- **Personal developments** (e.g., *The Creator has begun to hear the Navigator’s voice*).
- **Internal conflicts** (e.g., *The Rebel’s Firefighter part is clashing with their Manager*).

### **3. Update the Anomaly Log**
Track the **status of anomalies** in your world:
| Anomaly | Status | Discoverer | Notes |
|---------|--------|------------|-------|
| The Echoing Door | Unresolved | The Explorer | Last heard whispering "HELP ME." |
| The Whispering Stones | Partially Resolved | The Sage | One stone has gone silent. |

### **4. Reference Past Episodes**
- **Quote past dialogue** (e.g., *"Remember when the Navigator said, ‘The code is not as it seems’?"*).
- **Recall past events** (e.g., *"This anomaly behaves like the one you encountered in Session 2."*).
- **Foreshadow future connections** (e.g., *"The symbol on the door matches the one from your dream."*).

## 🤖 The Navigator as a Facilitator Tool

The **Navigator** (both as a Hidden God and as the AI tool in `ONTOSplayground`) can **assist the Facilitator** by:
1. **Suggesting Moves**: Recommending **Facilitator moves** based on the current situation.
   - Example: *"The party missed their roll. The Navigator suggests: ‘Announce future badness—the Debug Layer is destabilizing.’"*
2. **Generating Content**: Creating **new anomalies, NPCs, or threats** on the fly.
   - Example: *"The Navigator generates a new anomaly: *The Flickering Light*. It pulses with Morse code: ‘HELP ME.’"*
3. **Resolving Rolls**: Automating **dice rolls and move outcomes**.
   - Example: *"The Navigator rolls 2d6+Weird for *The Creator* and gets a 9. Partial success: the symbol carves itself, but the chisel breaks."*
4. **Narrating**: Providing **immersive descriptions** of events.
   - Example: *"The Navigator whispers: *The air smells like ozone. The door’s code is rewriting itself.*"*

### **Example Facilitator + Navigator Workflow**
1. **Player**: *"I want to investigate the Whispering Stones. I roll+Weird."*
2. **Facilitator**: *"Roll your dice."* (or lets the Navigator roll it)
3. **Navigator**: *"The roll is a 7. Partial success. The stones hum louder, but one of them cracks open, revealing a glowing symbol inside."*
4. **Facilitator**: *"The symbol matches the one from your dream last session. What do you do?"*

## 📌 Quick Reference: Facilitator Moves

| Move | When to Use |
|------|-------------|
| Separate them | The party is too united. |
| Capture someone | A character needs to be taken out of the scene. |
| Put someone in a spot | The party needs a challenge. |
| Trade harm for harm | Violence is escalating. |
| Announce off-screen badness | The world needs to feel alive. |
| Announce future badness | Foreshadowing is needed. |
| Inflict harm | A threat deals damage. |
| Take away their stuff | The party relies too much on a resource. |
| Make them buy | The party needs a tough choice. |
| Activate their stuff’s downside | A player’s gear or ability has a cost. |
| Tell them the consequences and ask | The party needs options. |
| Offer an opportunity | The party needs a chance. |
| Turn their move back on them | A player’s action has unintended consequences. |
| Make a threat move | A threat on your map takes action. |
