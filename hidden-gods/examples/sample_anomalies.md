## 🎲 **How to Use This Document**
This file contains **pre-generated anomalies** for each layer, ready to drop into your game. Each anomaly includes:
- **Name**: A short, evocative title.
- **Manifestation**: What the players see/hear/feel.
- **Clue**: A hint about the anomaly’s purpose or the simulation.
- **Purpose**: Why the anomaly exists (for the Facilitator).
- **Risk**: What happens if the players interact with it (usually a roll).

---

## 🌌 **Dream Layer Anomalies**

### **1. The Whispering Mirror**
- **Manifestation**: A full-length mirror that whispers when no one is looking at it. If a player looks directly into it, the whispers stop.
- **Clue**: *"The reflection blinks when you don’t."*
- **Purpose**: To test the players’ perception of self and reality.
- **Risk**: Roll+Weird to resist the mirror’s gaze. On a failure, the player sees their **true form** (a glitchy, distorted version of themselves) and takes **1-Weird** from the shock.

---

### **2. The Door That Wasn’t There**
- **Manifestation**: A door appears in a wall where there was none before. It’s slightly ajar, and warm light spills out.
- **Clue**: *"The air smells like childhood."*
- **Purpose**: To tempt the players into a **pocket dimension** (a memory or a lower layer).
- **Risk**: Roll+Cool to resist the urge to open it. On a failure, the player is **pulled into a memory** (Facilitator improvises a flashback scene).

---

### **3. The Clockwork Bird**
- **Manifestation**: A small, mechanical bird that sings a melody from the player’s past. It winds down as it sings, and its song grows slower and slower.
- **Clue**: *"The last note never ends."*
- **Purpose**: To reveal the **fluidity of time** in the Dream Layer.
- **Risk**: Roll+Sharp to understand the bird’s significance. On a success, the player gains a **clue about their past**. On a failure, they hear a **distorted version of their own voice** singing along.

---

### **4. The Endless Staircase**
- **Manifestation**: A spiral staircase that ascends and descends infinitely. The steps are made of a material that feels like **both stone and flesh**.
- **Clue**: *"The higher you climb, the lower you go."*
- **Purpose**: To symbolize the **cyclical nature of the simulation**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Cool to navigate the staircase. On a failure, the player **falls through the steps** and reappears at the bottom, disoriented (1-Cool).

---

### **5. The Book of Forgotten Names**
- **Manifestation**: A leather-bound book with no title. When opened, it contains the names of **people the players have never met**—but the names feel familiar.
- **Clue**: *"Your name is written on the last page."*
- **Purpose**: To hint at the **interconnectedness of all souls** in the simulation.
- **Risk**: Roll+Weird to read the book. On a success, the player learns a **secret about their past life**. On a failure, they **forget their own name** for the rest of the session (1-Weird to recall it).

## 🏙️ **Base Reality Anomalies**

### **1. The Man Who Wasn’t There**
- **Manifestation**: A man in a gray suit stands in the corner of the room, facing the wall. He doesn’t move, breathe, or acknowledge the players. If touched, he feels like **static**.
- **Clue**: *"No one else can see him."*
- **Purpose**: To reveal the **fragility of consensus reality**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Sharp to investigate. On a success, the player learns the man is a **glitch in the simulation**. On a failure, the man **turns to face them**—his face is a **blank screen** (1-Cool from the horror).

---

### **2. The Radio That Plays the Future**
- **Manifestation**: An old radio that plays **tomorrow’s news headlines**. The sound is staticky and cuts in and out.
- **Clue**: *"The battery is dead, but it still plays."*
- **Purpose**: To hint at the **predetermined nature of Base Reality**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Weird to change the future. On a success, the player can **alter one small event** in the next scene. On a failure, the radio **plays their own death** (1-Hot to keep going).

---

### **3. The Elevator to Nowhere**
- **Manifestation**: An elevator with buttons for floors that don’t exist in the building. Pressing a button makes the elevator **descend for hours** before the doors open to the same floor.
- **Clue**: *"The basement button is labeled 'DEBUG.'"*
- **Purpose**: To reveal the **layers beneath Base Reality**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Cool to resist the urge to press the DEBUG button. On a failure, the elevator **takes them to the Debug Layer** (whether they’re ready or not).

---

### **4. The Painting That Watches You**
- **Manifestation**: A portrait of a noble from the 18th century. The eyes follow the players, and the mouth **moves when they’re not looking**.
- **Clue**: *"The painter’s signature is your handwriting."*
- **Purpose**: To hint at the **player’s past lives** in the simulation.
- **Risk**: Roll+Charm to engage the painting in conversation. On a success, it reveals a **secret about the player’s past**. On a failure, it **screams** (1-Cool for all players in the room).

---

### **5. The Phone Call from Yourself**
- **Manifestation**: A player’s phone rings. The caller ID reads **"YOU"**. If answered, the voice on the other end is their own—but **older, tired, and desperate**.
- **Clue**: *"They say, 'Don’t make the same mistake I did.'"*
- **Purpose**: To foreshadow the **cyclical nature of the simulation**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Sharp to decipher the message. On a success, the player gains a **clue about avoiding a future mistake**. On a failure, the call **ends with a dial tone that never stops** (1-Weird to ignore it).

---

## 💻 **Debug Layer Anomalies**

### **1. The Floating Equation**
- **Manifestation**: A **3D mathematical equation** hovers in midair, its symbols shifting and rearranging. Touching it causes the symbols to **burn into your skin**.
- **Clue**: *"The equation solves for 'you.'"*
- **Purpose**: To reveal the **mathematical nature of reality** in the Debug Layer.
- **Risk**: Roll+Sharp to solve the equation. On a success, the player learns a **hidden truth about their role in the simulation**. On a failure, the equation **rearranges into a curse** (1-Sharp to break it).

---

### **2. The Door with No Handle**
- **Manifestation**: A door with no handle, lock, or seam. It’s **completely smooth**, but a faint **humming** comes from behind it.
- **Clue**: *"The door is the same temperature as your skin."*
- **Purpose**: To test the players’ ability to **interact with the impossible**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Weird to open the door. On a success, it leads to a **random layer**. On a failure, the door **absorbs their hand** (1-Weird to pull it free).

---

### **3. The Voice in the Static**
- **Manifestation**: A **terminal window** appears, displaying static. After a few seconds, a voice begins speaking through the static—**it’s the player’s own voice**, but distorted.
- **Clue**: *"It’s reciting your last words before you die."*
- **Purpose**: To hint at the **predetermined nature of the Debug Layer**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Cool to resist the voice’s influence. On a success, the player can **ask the voice one question**. On a failure, the voice **repeats their next words before they say them** (1-Cool to break the loop).

---

### **4. The Grid That Isn’t There**
- **Manifestation**: The ground **flickers** between solid floor and a **grid of floating hexagons**. Stepping on a hexagon causes it to **sink slightly**, as if it’s a button.
- **Clue**: *"The pattern repeats every 7 steps."*
- **Purpose**: To test the players’ **spatial reasoning** in a non-Euclidean space.
- **Risk**: Roll+Sharp to navigate the grid. On a success, the players find a **hidden path**. On a failure, the grid **rearranges**, and they must roll again or take **1-Cool** to force their way through.

---

### **5. The God in the Code**
- **Manifestation**: A **section of the wall** peels back to reveal a **mass of writhing code**. At its center is a **pair of glowing eyes**—The Debugger, watching.
- **Clue**: *"It blinks in Morse code: 'HELP ME.'"*
- **Purpose**: To introduce the **Hidden God of the Debug Layer**.
- **Risk**: Roll+Charm to negotiate. On a success, The Debugger **offers a bargain**. On a failure, it **rewrites a section of the player’s code** (1-Weird to resist).

## 🛠️ **Facilitator Tips**
- **Customize Anomalies**: Change the **clues or risks** to fit your table’s story.
- **Combine Anomalies**: Mix and match manifestations and clues for **unique effects**.
- **Use Anomalies to Foreshadow**: Drop clues about **future layers or gods**.
- **Let Players Create Anomalies**: Ask them, *"What glitch do you see?"* and incorporate their ideas.
